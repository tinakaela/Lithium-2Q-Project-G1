Experts Reveal How Climate-Driven Flooding Has Slashed Australian Home Values by $42 Billion

By: Reno Sobrepena

A recent report from the Climate Council and property data company PropTrack reveals that flooding has significantly impacted the Australian housing market, leading to a decrease in property values by approximately AU$42.2 billion (around US$28 to 30 billion).

The study highlights that roughly two million homes located in flood-prone areas across the country have experienced a median loss of about US$75,000 each. Alarmingly, 70% of these properties belong to low-income families, which deepens existing social and economic inequalities.

Queensland and New South Wales have been particularly hard hit, with certain suburbs such as Chelmer in Brisbane seeing home values drop by more than 10%, translating to losses of around AU$303,000 per affected property.

Experts attribute these losses to a combination of poor urban planning, outdated infrastructure, and a lack of consideration for evolving climate risks. They emphasize the urgent need for improved flood mapping, investment in resilient infrastructure, and stronger government policies to manage development in vulnerable locations.

This situation serves as a clear reminder that climate change is not just an environmental crisis but also a profound financial and social challenge. Many homeowners, often with limited resources, are bearing the brunt of the consequences resulting from insufficient climate action.
