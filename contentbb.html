World’s First Personalized CRISPR Therapy Treats Infant with Rare Disorder

By: Martina Almaden

Doctors at the Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia (CHOP) have successfully treated the first patient using a personalized CRISPR gene-editing therapy.

The patient, a baby boy named KJ, was born with Carbamoyl-Phosphate Synthetase 1 (CPS1) deficiency, a rare disorder that prevents the body from removing ammonia.

The mutation posed a great threat to his life, as high ammonia levels can cause brain damage or even death.

To treat him, researchers from CHOP and Penn Medicine developed a custom CRISPR therapy designed for his unique genetic mutation.

The treatment used base editing, a newer CRISPR method that corrects a single DNA letter without cutting the DNA strand.

It was delivered to his liver cells using lipid nanoparticles.

After receiving the therapy, he showed major improvement in his condition.

He can now tolerate more protein, needs fewer medications, and recovers from illnesses without dangerous ammonia spikes.

According to CHOP, this is the first time a fully personalized, in-body CRISPR treatment has been used in a human.
