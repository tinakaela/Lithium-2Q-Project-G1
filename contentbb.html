World’s First Personalized CRISPR Therapy Treats Infant with Rare Disorder

By: Martina Almaden

Doctors at the Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia (CHOP) have successfully treated the first patient using a fully personalized CRISPR therapy, marking a major milestone in precision medicine.

The patient, a baby boy named KJ with the rare metabolic disorder CPS1 deficiency, received a treatment tailored specifically to his one-of-a-kind genetic mutation.

This disorder prevents the body from removing ammonia, making even small buildups potentially fatal for infants.

To address this, researchers from CHOP and Penn Medicine engineered a custom base-editing therapy designed to correct a single faulty DNA letter without cutting the DNA.

They delivered the editor to his liver cells using lipid nanoparticles, a method that allows safer, more targeted gene editing inside the body.

After treatment, KJ showed remarkable improvement, tolerating more protein, relying on fewer medications, and recovering from illness without dangerous ammonia spikes.

According to researchers, this success demonstrates that gene editing can be individualized to a patient’s exact mutation rather than relying on one-size-fits-all therapies.

Aditionally, this breakthrough could reshape the future of medicine by enabling rapid, personalized treatments for countless rare disorders.
