Fighting the Invisible Enemy: Pandemic Preparedness and AMR

By: Andrei Soberano

The COVID-19 pandemic showed us how important it is to be prepared for health emergencies. Pandemic preparedness means being ready before an outbreak happens by strengthening healthcare systems, improving public awareness, and supporting scientific research for vaccines and treatments.

Another big global issue is Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR). This happens when bacteria, viruses, and other microbes change and no longer respond to medicines like antibiotics. Because of this, infections that used to be easy to cure become harder and more dangerous. AMR usually happens when people misuse or overuse antibiotics, or when hospitals and communities lack proper hygiene.

Pandemics and AMR are connected because during pandemics, people often use antibiotics too much, which can increase resistance. This is why being prepared, preventing infections, and using medicines responsibly are so important.

As students, we can help by washing our hands regularly, following doctors’ prescriptions, supporting vaccination, and spreading awareness about public health.

Being ready for pandemics and fighting AMR is not just the job of doctors or scientists — it’s everyone’s responsibility. Together, we can build a healthier and safer future for all
